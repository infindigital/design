The price of the giant flags: replaced every month, a bigger bill with each new tender

Five hundred monumental flag installations were estimated to cost around ₹200 crore to install and maintain. We combed through three consecutive tenders to reveal why long-term estimates have soared — even as unit costs were claimed to fall.

SG

Samarth Grover

Staff Reporter · Published Oct 26, 2024 · 8 min read

Share close thumb_up chat

A monumental flag installation on a central dais. Photo illustration · The Clarion

When the civic body first announced a plan to raise 500 monumental flags across the city, the gesture was framed as a matter of pride. Two years and three tenders later, the accounting tells a more complicated story — one of fabric that frays within weeks, estimates that balloon between bids, and a maintenance bill that quietly outgrows the cost of the flags themselves.

Documents reviewed by The Clarion show that the long-term budget projection rose from an initial ₹140 crore to just under ₹200 crore across successive rounds, even as the stated per-unit replacement cost was revised downward. The apparent contradiction, officials concede, comes down to one line item: how often each flag must be swapped.

Why the fabric does not last

In areas where the air quality index routinely crosses 400, the polyester weave used for the installations degrades far faster than the original specification assumed. Internal notes attached to the second tender recommend a mandatory 30-day replacement cycle in the worst-affected zones — a schedule that multiplies annual fabric spend by a factor of three.

"The flag is not the expense. The expense is the calendar on which the flag must die and be reborn."

That calendar, buried in an annexure, is where the money goes. A single installation in a high-pollution corridor can consume a dozen replacement panels a year, each requiring a crane, a crew and a traffic diversion.

The 42% gap

More striking is the spread between bids. Across the maintenance consortium proposals we examined, quoted rates deviated from the municipal baseline estimate by as much as 42% — a range wide enough that officials privately describe the figures as "difficult to compare like for like."

What our review found

  • check_circle Fabric degradation in severe AQI conditions triggers mandatory 30-day swaps in the worst zones.
  • check_circle Maintenance bids showed up to 42% deviation from the baseline municipal estimate.
  • check_circle Long-term projections rose ~₹60 crore even as unit replacement costs were revised down.

The second tender introduced a clause allowing the contractor to invoice for "weather-driven emergency replacements" outside the fixed schedule — an open-ended category that accounts for a growing share of recent claims.

"Unit costs fell. The bill went up. Both statements are true — and that is the whole problem." — A procurement official, speaking on condition of anonymity

What happens next

A third tender, now in its evaluation stage, proposes shifting to a treated weave the manufacturer claims will double panel life. If the claim holds, it could flatten the replacement curve that has driven costs. If it does not, the city will have locked in a higher baseline for a problem it has yet to solve.

The civic body did not respond to a detailed questionnaire before publication. We will update this report if a response is received.
